
===== mania-hub
mania-hub (mania-tracker.com) is a TanStack Start/React 19 frontend. The part that matters, `live-backend/` (ingest, SQLite schema, job queue, the MinaCalc WASM, vendored LeoBlack, replay judge), is gitignored and not public (AGENTS.md:6, :54). What you can read is the frontend, docs/, scripts/, and an `algorithms/` snapshot. Only that snapshot carries a license: MIT, algorithms/LICENSE. The repo root has no license, and the GitHub API reports license=null. Everything outside algorithms/ is all-rights-reserved, so ideas only. The snapshot cannot run on its own: it imports `leoblack/*` and declares the Wife-model coefficients without shipping them (wife-calibration.ts:37-54). Activity: one author, 1,954 commits, created 2026-03-30, last push today.

Scores come mainly from a third-party feed, osu! Score Cache (osc.kaysting.dev), over Socket.IO plus JSON backfill. The osu! API v2 is a fallback poller and is used for enrichment, rosters, top-200 and replay downloads, behind a 45/min target and a 60/min hard limit.

Difficulty sources:
- MinaCalc WASM: 0.72.3 for 4K, 0.75.0 for 5K-18K, with a per-skillset clamp raised 40 to 100 (declared, not verifiable).
- LeoBlack Sunny SR mapped through LeoBlack's 7K interval tables. This gives the 7K dan scale (7K Regular Gamma = Sunny 8.537-8.99).
- A 4K-only independent LN model.

For 7K LN, the rating is MinaCalc Overall with a 30% heuristic blend toward a "tails-as-taps" pass. No real 7K LN model exists.

Player skill uses Etterna's approach. Each play's accuracy is converted to an estimated Wife3 goal, MinaCalc computes the SSR at that goal and rate, and the best play per chart/rate slot (capped at 2 per chart family) is combined with Etterna's AggregateSSRs. The 7K "pattern" ratings are just aggregated Overall SSRs of plays on charts carrying that tag, not true per-pattern skill. Dan per side is the mean of skill tiles, each tile averaging its best 20 credited clears, with course and practice-pack clears as floors. History is a per-day change log. Percentiles come from shrunk population quantile curves.

Training: no model learns chart difficulty from player outcomes. No IRT, Elo or Glicko. What was fitted:
- A nonnegative least-squares model mapping judgment counts to Wife3, validated with player and chart-family holdouts (7K n is small).
- A power-law LN scale fitted on 9 odd-level 4K LN dan courses.
- A 6-feature logistic model splitting 4K speed from tech (AUC 0.883).
- Many thresholds tuned against labelled charts.
- A kNN over 132 reference 4K LN charts.
- A pre-registered AUC test of the 7K LN axis with 8 vs 5 hand labels.

Missing entirely: hand-layout awareness, per-pattern diagnosis from replays, per-session improvement analysis, practice-section cutting, and pattern-targeted recommendations (the "farm helper" is pp-peer based). Their public API currently rates TWulfZ at 7K rice 9-- and LN 9, a usable external baseline.

-- DESIGN
 * Use mania-tracker only as an external baseline and sanity check, not as a dependency. The public read-only /api/profiles/:id/skill-history?keys=7 lets wolluf show 'mania-tracker says 9-- / LN 9' next to its own estimate. Respect their public rate limit, cache the result, and fetch once per session at most.
 * Calibrate against dan courses and practice packs, not against their numbers. Anchor wolluf's per-axis difficulty scale on Jinjin 7K courses and skill practice packs (0th to Stellium) the same way they anchored 4K LN on course levels. Treat LeoBlack's MIT 7K Sunny interval tables (Regular Gamma = Sunny 8.537-8.99) as an MIT prior for the overall 7K dan mapping.
 * Port only MIT pieces, with attribution: Etterna AggregateSSRs (skill-ratings.ts:878-895), the extrapolation above the 0.965 goal cap, the dan-credit accuracy curve shape (dan-credit.ts), and the slot rules (best per chart/rate, at most 2 per chart family). Each should be a small documented function that wolluf's own code calls.
 * Don't estimate Wife3 from judgment counts. wolluf has local .osr replays, so it should compute Wife3 (and per-note offsets) exactly by re-judging frames. mania-hub's own docs show the count-based estimate has 0.15-0.9 pp MAE on 7K and cannot separate head from tail error on LN. Use a count-based estimate only for plays with no replay (tosu fail capture).
 * Don't copy their 7K pattern-rating method (Overall SSR aggregated over tagged charts). wolluf's per-axis IRT/Glicko must attribute outcomes at section or pattern level, so every observation is a (pattern-window, difficulty, measured Wife/miss) triple from the replay. That is exactly the gap mania-hub has.
 * Keep their gating rules for data hygiene: goal floor 0.8, refuse HO/NR, handle DA/EZ/HR windows, exclude vibro/mash charts from rating, and cap rate stacking per note-verified chart family. Without these, the player model will be inflated by farm and vibro plays.
 * Model 7K LN properly. mania-hub's 7K LN is MinaCalc Overall with a 30% tail blend, which is a known weak spot the author plans to rework. wolluf's deterministic engine should model release timing and holds-under-taps per hand, using the user-configured layout (for TWulfZ: left 1-3, thumb on 4, right 5-7), not a floor/ceil(k/2) split.
 * Resolve the axis vocabulary before freezing the schema. mania-hub and its practice registry use 7K Regular Jack/Tech/Speed/Stream, not Stamina. Make the axis set data-driven (config per keymode) so 7K and a future 4K (jack/tech/speed/stamina) both fit.
 * Session tracker: mirror their durable per-day change log (store only deltas on change, freeze the display baseline, keep model-version changes as separate 'system' events). Add what they lack: per-session per-axis posterior deltas with credible intervals, so 'improved' and 'got worse' are not noise.
 * Validation protocol worth borrowing: pre-register the test, hold out players and chart families separately, report exposure baselines (LN play share) and placebos, and run permutation AUC. Apply it to wolluf's axes before claiming they diagnose weaknesses.
 * Data sourcing: local-first (scores.db, Data/r replays, tosu for fails) plus osu! API only for the logged-in user's own data at irregular intervals. Don't build a global score harvester. That avoids the grey area of the 'mass harvesting' clause entirely.

===== replay-rejudge
Stable osu!mania replays can be re-judged per note. I tested this on the user's own 4,363 local 7K replays: 4,272 complete plays, 90 failed plays, 1 file that would not decompress. Each replay was matched to its map through osu!.db, the map file's MD5 was checked, the play was re-judged and the result compared with the judgement counts in the replay header.

**Rice matches very well.**
- No mods, ScoreV1: 681/693 replays match exactly (98.3%).
- Mirror: 30/30. DT/NC/HT: 25/25, once the windows are converted to map time as floor(window × rate).
- ScoreV2 with HR: 86%. After the HR fixes, the rice mismatches that remain are almost all one or two notes.
- Two findings that differ from docs and existing ports:
  - Under HR, stable shrinks every window by 1/1.4 except the early-miss window (188 − 3·OD), which stays unscaled. This took ScoreV2+HR from 2/40 to 32/40 exact.
  - The ScoreV2 MAX window must be floored with a small epsilon: in floating point, 24.9 − 1.1×9 comes out as 14.999…, which floors to the wrong value.

**LN is only approximate.** ScoreV1 on LN-heavy maps: 3–16% of replays match exactly, and at least 0.2–0.45% of judgements differ. ScoreV2 LN is the user's actual LN mode (mods = 536870912): 4% exact, at least 0.41% of judgements differ. The windows are not the problem: for ScoreV2 tails, |release offset| / 1.5 is a sharp best fit, and 1.4 or 1.6 are far worse. What nobody has documented is what happens when an LN is released mid-body and pressed again, and when a key is held past the tail. The osu! wiki's rule ("releasing during the body caps at MEH") does not reproduce stable's counts. The ravinyan/osuReplayAnalyzer author ran into the same thing (an LN released mid-body still got a MAX judgement).

**Base to port:** Interlude/prelude (YAVSRG, MIT, F#) has a stable-parity ruleset tested against real stable replays. Its notelock rule (earliest unhit note; a press is ignored if an already-hit note in that column is still in the future) and its late edge (OK − 1 ms) reproduce stable exactly for rice. It has no ScoreV2 model and no fix for HR's miss window. Companella's matcher is looser (late window = miss window, its own notelock rule) and is not suitable for exact matching.

**Pitfalls:**
- osrparse throws away the two lead-in frames before adding up time deltas, so every time comes out +1 ms off.
- No audio or universal offset needs to be applied: parity is exact without any.
- Random mod: stable's shuffle algorithm is not public.

**Contradicts one of the brief's premises:** on the user's current client (version 20260924), failed plays are saved. All 90 incomplete replays are in scores.db, and their life-bar graphs end at an HP of 0.01 or less.

Scripts are in /tmp/claude-1000/-home-twulfz-dev-wolluf/879b26ab-9465-45fd-b47b-2a469d1d893f/scratchpad/rejudge/: rejudge.py, osudb.py, batch.py, summ2.py, and batch_5000_*.json with the results.

-- DESIGN
 * Build wolluf's re-judging engine in Rust by porting Interlude prelude's osu!mania ruleset (MIT). Add the fixes found here: HR leaves the MISS window unscaled; rate-mod windows are floor(base*rate) in map time; floor with an epsilon; ScoreV1+HR MAX window depends on OD; a separate ScoreV2 model (head and tail judged separately, tail |offset|/1.5, late cut at the 100 edge, cap 50 after a break).
 * Parse .osr yourself: LZMA_ALONE, accumulate absolute time over all frames including the lead-in frames, then apply lazer's frame fix-ups. Do not rely on osrparse's time_delta values (+1 ms error).
 * Resolve maps through osu!.db by MD5 (≈0.5 s for 22k maps) and check the .osu file's MD5 before judging. Only scan Songs/ as a fallback for maps missing from osu!.db.
 * For each replay, store per-note records (column, map time, kind note/head/tail, delta_ms, judgement, flags for break/overhold/regrab), plus a 'parity' field comparing re-judged counts with the header. Record a parity class per replay (exact / ≤2 notes / worse) and weight or exclude replays from player-model calibration based on it.

===== map-cutting-drills
Two ways of making osu!mania practice content work on stable. Neither needs a practice mode inside the game client. (A) An overlay cut: a new .osu with only its [HitObjects] filtered, which keeps the original audio file and every timing point. cosu-trainer (GPL-3.0), Companella's Difficulty Splitter (MIT) and osu-practice-tool (MIT) all work this way. (B) A rendered drill: the audio is trimmed, faded, sped up or slowed down and joined into a new file, and all .osu times are shifted to match. Only Companella's Marathon Creator does this, by calling ffmpeg as a separate process. Its timing-point handling has two defects that wolluf must not copy: it drops the SV (scroll velocity) that is active at the cut point and moves the BPM line to the first note, which breaks the beat phase. Its storyboard Sample (keysound) events are never shifted. osu-trainer has no license (all rights reserved), so its pipeline (NAudio decode, soundstretch, LAME, whole folder zipped into an .osz and opened with the shell) can only be used as a reference. Stable has no looping, so N repetitions have to be written into the audio by joining copies. Per-rep accuracy then has to come from re-judging the replay, because scores.db stores one score per beatmap MD5, and a failed play is saved neither as a score nor as a replay. Every edit changes the MD5, so wolluf needs a registry that maps each drill MD5 to the source MD5, section, rate, repetitions and time offsets. The .osr also carries the beatmap MD5, so replays map back to the source too. In-client practice modes in other games give usable numbers. Etterna loops a region with 2 s of music before it and 5 s after, and resets the notes on each loop. Interlude ignores notes for the first 800 ms/rate after the practice point. Stable adds at least 1.8 s of lead-in and skips the intro when you retry with Ctrl+R. From the user's install: 18,905 7K difficulties. 94.9% use mp3 and 4.8% ogg. About 5–6% are keysounded, which makes changing their rate harder. The user already practises dan maps with osu-trainer rate ladders in steps of about 0.04–0.05x. The two synthetic generators found are small, have no license, rely on random rules and do not support LN. I found no community evidence for synthetic versus real sections, because the web-search budget ran out. For Rust: Symphonia (MPL-2.0, gapless mp3 and vorbis) to decode, signalsmith-stretch (MIT) to change speed without changing pitch, rubato (MIT) to change speed and pitch together, and vorbis_rs (BSD-3) to write .ogg output. This avoids LAME's LGPL and the mp3 encoder-delay problem when writing files. Stable ships BASS 2.4.15, which removes MP3 decoder delay, but whether it honours LAME's gapless tag is unverified. The whole pipeline should therefore be calibrated against replay hit-error data.

-- DESIGN
 * Ship two drill types. (1) 'Overlay cut' at 1.0x: no audio work. It is a new .osu in a wolluf-owned folder, or in the source folder if the user opts in, with the same AudioFilename, all timing points kept, HitObjects filtered to the window, and PreviewTime set to the window start. It relies on stable's Skip and Ctrl+R (which skips the intro) and is ready in under a second. (2) 'Rendered drill': a new folder with trimmed, sped-up or joined audio for rates other than 1.0x, repetitions, and context pre-roll.
 * Rendered pipeline: decode the source once with Symphonia to f32 PCM, then trim with pre-roll and post-roll, stretch (signalsmith-stretch to keep pitch, rubato resampling to change pitch with rate), apply short fades (about 0.3–1 s), add silent gaps, join N reps, and encode once. Default output is Ogg Vorbis via vorbis_rs (BSD, sample-accurate, playable by stable). mp3 via LAME stays optional, dynamically linked for LGPL compliance. Avoid an ffmpeg sidecar, so there is no GPL/LGPL binary to distribute and no subprocess fragility.
 * Rewriting .osu timing: t' = O_k + (t − (a − p))/r. For each rep, emit (i) the uninherited point active at a − p, placed at the first beat (or downbeat, using meter) at or after a − p so beat phase and bar lines are kept, with beatLength/r; (ii) the inherited SV point active at a − p, re-emitted at the same time; (iii) every timing point inside the window, shifted, with red-line beatLength divided by r and green-line SV values unchanged. Do not copy Companella's 'BPM point at first note, drop SV' shortcut.
 * Hit objects: keep notes whose head is in [a,b]. Extend the window end to cover the tail of every kept LN, never clamp tails as Companella does, because clamping changes release difficulty. Drop LNs whose head is before a. Snap a and b to downbeats that fall in gaps between notes, and never split a chord across the boundary.
 * Events and samples: shift Break and storyboard 'Sample,' events (Companella doesn't). Copy every custom hitsound or keysound file the kept objects reference into the new folder. For keysounded maps (~5–6% of the user's 7K library), only allow 1.0x, or turn off rate changes, until per-sample stretching exists.
 * Header: Version follows a readable template such as '<orig diff> ‹wolluf› <axis/pattern> <m:ss–m:ss> <rate>x ×<reps>'. Tags gets 'wolluf drill <axis> <drillId>' appended, since the Tags search trick already works for osu-trainer. BeatmapID:0 and BeatmapSetID:-1 in wolluf-owned folders. AudioLeadIn:0 with at least 2 s of real pre-roll music, which matches Etterna's 2 s and beats stable's 1.8 s minimum. PreviewTime = O_0 + p/r. Offer a lower HPDrainRate or recommend NF, because failed plays are never saved.
 * Import: write the folder and .osz and open the .osz through the shell, as osu-trainer and cosu do, so a running stable imports it immediately. Fall back to writing the folder into Songs and asking the user to press F5. Keep a manifest of everything wolluf generated so cleanup only deletes wolluf files, and never touch non-wolluf files.
 * Registry: compute the MD5 of the exact bytes written and store drill_md5 → {source_md5, a, b, p, rate, reps, O_k[], pattern_cluster_ids, generator_version}. Map every .osr or tosu event on a drill back to source time with t_src = (a − p) + (t − O_k)·r. Per-rep and per-pattern accuracy comes from wolluf's own replay re-judging, never from the single scores.db row.
 * Offset self-calibration: after the first rendered drill of a source, compare mean hit error on the drill's replay with the user's mean error on the source map. A constant shift of about 10–30 ms means the decoder gapless policy differs from BASS. Store a per-codec correction and bake it into the .osu times. BASS is 2.4.15, and whether it honours the LAME tag is unverified.
 * Drill length per axis (a heuristic, not validated): speed, jack and tech clusters get 10–30 s windows × 3–5 reps with short gaps. Stamina (Regular) and general LN use a single 60–120 s pass with no reps, because chopping stamina defeats its purpose. For release and inverse LN, the window end must include full tails plus about 1 beat.
 * Rate ladder: start at the rate where the model predicts about target − 2pp accuracy on the pattern. Step ±0.05x, which matches the user's existing habit. Promote when 2 of the last 3 reps at that rate reach the target accuracy on the pattern notes. Demote after 2 reps at or below target − 3pp. Count the section as 'mastered' only after a transfer check: the same section played inside the full map at 1.0x reaches the target in a later session. Treat all thresholds as parameters to calibrate on the user's data.
 * Prefer real-map cuts over synthetic generation for the MVP. If synthetic drills are added later, generate them from the user's weak-pattern templates on top of a real map's rhythm and audio, as OsuManiaMan does, with LN support and awareness of the user's hand layout. Neither existing generator provides these.

===== session-skill-tracking
Prior art converges on one session rule and fails on the skill tracker itself. Tachi and Interlude both split sessions after a 2-hour idle gap. Tachi does it so a session isn't cut in two across timezones, and because after 2 hours the player is "probably no longer warm". Interlude also keeps a separate calendar day that starts at 04:00. None of the trackers I read separates skill from noise. Tachi's session value is the average of the best 10 ratings, returned as null under 10 scores; its "improvement" is a delta against the previous PB and lamp/folder raises. Etterna's rating history is an aggregate of the best score per chart as of each date, and adding scores never lowers it, so it cannot show a regression. Companella fits a line through the MSD of the maps a player chose (min 5 plays, 0.95 recency weight), which tracks map choice rather than skill, with no uncertainty. mania-hub keeps a daily snapshot, a 3-play minimum per pattern, and a dated note wherever a rating-model change moved the numbers. Interlude removed its pattern skill ratings in 0.7.28.3. Changelog reasons: bugs that could break the scale, confusion with the score-screen performance rating, and too-tight coupling to the pattern analyser. The commit adds "it may never come back". The removed code shows why: it credited whole-chart accuracy to every pattern cluster, kept a records frontier that could only go up, and used hand-tuned multipliers with no uncertainty. Lichess gets small-sample handling right: a theme counts as weak only with 3+ failures and strong only with 3+ first-try wins, and themed puzzle training moves the rating less (weight 0.7-0.8). Glicko adds rating uncertainty (RD) for inactive time and gives 95% intervals. Motor-learning research separates performance during practice from learning shown on a later retention test. Sleep consolidation (Walker 2002: about 20% faster motor speed after a night's sleep) means end-of-session numbers understate skill. Mental fatigue lowers accuracy and reaction time, and timing variability grows with time on task. Proposed spec for wolluf: keep raw per-note replay observations and recompute skill when the pattern engine changes. Model each axis as a Bayesian ability θ_a with uncertainty σ_a, updated from how each segment's accuracy differs from what its difficulty predicts, with a shared per-play offset so segment counts don't inflate confidence. Between sessions σ grows as in Glicko and the mean does not decay. A session may only say "improved" with enough evidence and a credible interval, and "confirmed" only after the next session repeats it. "Regressed" needs 2+ sessions with no fatigue flag. Fatigue within a session is flagged with CUSUM/EWMA charts on accuracy residuals and on timing spread.

-- DESIGN
 * STORAGE (the lesson from Interlude's coupling): keep raw per-note observations permanently. Per play: chart_hash, rate, mods, OD, keymode, layout_id. Per note: column, t_ms, offset_ms, judgement, ln_head/ln_tail, release_offset_ms. For failed plays, also the tosu fail/quit time. Store pattern-engine output (segment labels and per-axis difficulty) in a separate table keyed by engine_version. Skill state (μ_a, σ_a) is a derived cache that can be rebuilt from scratch when the engine or calibrator changes. Every snapshot carries model_version, and changes get dated notes in the mania-hub style.
 * SESSION SEGMENTATION: start a new session when the gap from last play end to next play start exceeds T_session = 120 min (Tachi and Interlude both use 2h; configurable 30-240). Inside a session, start a new 'block' after a pause of more than T_block = 10 min (wolluf proposal, not sourced); it resets the fatigue baseline and marks the next ~5 min as warm-up. Calendar grouping uses a 04:00 local day boundary (Interlude). Detect sessions automatically, with manual merge, split and rename (Companella is manual-only; Tachi can't merge sessions after a gap closes). Record retries, quits and fail positions from tosu, since stable scores.db has no failed plays.
 * PER-AXIS UNIT OF EVIDENCE: a pattern-engine segment (a contiguous run of one axis label, about 2-8 s at the played rate) with calibrated difficulty d_{s,a}. Primary metric: residual r_s = y_s − ŷ_s, where y_s is the segment's judgement-point accuracy under the played OD and ŷ_s = f(μ_a − d_{s,a}) is the prediction from ability and difficulty. This measures skill independent of map choice, which Companella's MSD-vs-time slope does not. Only the segment's own notes count toward its axis, never whole-chart accuracy (Interlude's flaw).
 * SECONDARY METRICS per axis, per session. (a) Per-finger timing bias = median offset, spread = 1.4826·MAD, excluding misses. Columns map to fingers and hands through the user's layout config; TWulfZ's layout is L-hand cols 1-3, R-thumb col 4, R fingers cols 5-7, and every stat is also shown per hand/thumb. (b) LN: head bias and spread, release bias and spread, early-release rate, late-release rate. Inverse: gap-release timing. (c) Miss clustering: dispersion index D = var/mean of misses per 1 s window; D > 1.5 marks a choke section. A section that fails in ≥2 attempts becomes a drill candidate. (d) Consistency: SD of the same section's residuals across attempts. (e) Stamina: residual slope from the first to the last third of a play on matched difficulty. (f) Behaviour: retry and quit rate, time on each axis.
 * PLAYER MODEL: θ_a ~ N(μ_a, σ_a²) for a ∈ {jack, tech, speed, stamina, ln_general, ln_tech, inverse, release}, per keymode. Updates run per play as a Laplace/Kalman (Glicko-like) step on that play's segments for axis a. A shared per-play offset u_p ~ N(0, τ_p²) caps each play's information at 1/τ_p², so 200 segments from one play can't look like 200 independent tests. Glicko-2 warns that fewer than 10-15 results per period is thin evidence. Scale θ in Jinjin 7K dan units, the same scale as chart difficulty. One family of numbers avoids Interlude's 'totally different system' confusion.
 * DRILL / RETRY WEIGHTING: repeated attempts on the same cut section, and self-selected pattern drills, leak information. Lichess scales themed training by 0.7-0.8. Proposal: weight = 0.7 for drills, then 0.7^k for the k-th repeat of the same section within a session. Drill-only gains stay 'provisional' until they show up on unseen charts. Exclude vibro-exploitable sections, as mania-hub does, and plays with HO/NR mods.
 * RUST MODEL: between sessions, σ_a² ← min(σ_a² + c_a²·Δdays, σ_prior²). Calibrate c_a with Glickman's method, e.g. 180 idle days to return from a typical σ to the prior. Refit it from data to maximise next-session log-likelihood. Do not decay the mean; Interlude's unused ln(0.9)/day constant is not a model. Model warm-up instead: a covariate w·1[t_in_block < 5 min] absorbs early-block underperformance, so it is not read as rust or regression.
 * DECISION RULES per axis per session. EVIDENCE GATE: ≥3 plays containing the axis (mania-hub and lichess convention) and ≥20 segments (≥60 s of axis time) with |d − μ_a| ≤ 1.5 dan. Below that, show 'not enough data' explicitly. PROVISIONAL IMPROVEMENT: P(μ_post − μ_pre > δ) ≥ 0.80, reported with an 80% credible interval. CONFIRMED: the gain holds on the next session's first non-warm-up plays, and P(θ_now − θ_14d_ago > δ) ≥ 0.90 (retention test per Kantak & Winstein). REGRESSED: only when P(drop > δ) ≥ 0.90 across ≥2 sessions and no fatigue flag. A single-session drop is labelled 'off day / fatigue', never 'got worse'. δ = max(0.1 dan, 1.96·√2·SEM_a), with SEM_a from test-retest of play residuals (reliable-change logic).
 * FATIGUE / TILT DETECTION within a block, after excluding warm-up. (1) Accuracy drift: one-sided CUSUM on standardized play residuals −z_p with k = 0.5, h = 4. (2) Timing spread growth: EWMA (λ = 0.3, L = 3) on log(SD_p/SD_baseline) (Reteig 2019: variability rises with time on task). (3) EWMA of retry/quit rate. (4) Rising miss-clustering index. Fatigue = (1) or (2) firing after ≥30 min with gradual onset. Tilt = ≥3 quits in the first 20% of a chart within 5 min of a fail — a heuristic, labelled low-confidence. Response: suggest a break or easier maps, keep the plays, and mark the session effect so it doesn't pull θ down.
 * SESSION REPORT. Header: duration, plays, time per axis, warm-up and fatigue markers on a timeline. Then three lists capped at 2-3 items each: Improved (provisional or confirmed, e.g. '+0.25 dan, 80% CrI [+0.08, +0.41], from 18 jack segments in 5 plays'), Regressed (confirmed only), Practice next. Practice-next score = (target_a − μ_a)/σ_scale × relevance to the user's goal (Gamma rice/LN) × evidence factor. Each item names concrete sections: the most negative-residual sections from recent plays, cut as drills at a rate where predicted accuracy is about 93-96%, with a finger-level hint (e.g. 'thumb col 4 +7 ms late on jacks'). Show causes only when the per-finger stat's interval excludes 0. Always say 'not enough data' rather than hiding axes. Never show max-only records (Etterna/Interlude frontiers) as skill, because they can't show a regression.
 * SPACED REVIEW (analogy, low confidence): track a per-axis 'last solid practice' date. Schedule review drills for recently improved axes, preferring moderate rust over daily hammering (FSRS stability gain rises as R falls). Validate this with TWulfZ's own data before surfacing it to other users.

-- MISSING
